Uganda prepares for nationwide National Cleaning Day as communities are urged to improve sanitation

Friday, 30 October 2026 · Infinity Media Network News Desk

Ugandans across the country are preparing to take part in the fourth National Cleaning Day on Saturday, 31 October 2026, in a coordinated effort aimed at improving sanitation, protecting the environment and creating cleaner public spaces.

The nationwide exercise is scheduled to run from 7:00 a.m. to 10:00 a.m. and is expected to involve residents, local leaders, businesses, schools, public institutions and community organisations. The activity is planned to take place across districts, cities, municipalities, town councils, parishes and villages, making it a countrywide call for collective responsibility.

The exercise is intended to focus attention on places where poor waste management can create health and environmental problems. These include roadsides, markets, drainage channels, trading centres, school compounds, health facilities, workplaces and other shared public spaces. Residents are being encouraged to take part in cleaning their immediate surroundings and helping keep commonly used areas safe and orderly.

Why the exercise matters

Uncollected rubbish and blocked drainage channels can contribute to flooding, unpleasant surroundings and conditions that allow disease-carrying pests to multiply. Plastic waste and other materials thrown into open drains can obstruct the flow of rainwater, while poorly managed waste can affect the appearance and usability of markets, roads and neighbourhoods.

Regular cleaning and responsible disposal of rubbish are therefore important not only for the appearance of towns and villages, but also for public health and environmental protection. The campaign is designed to remind communities that sanitation is a shared responsibility rather than a task that belongs only to municipal workers.

Communities and local leaders expected to take part

Local authorities and community leaders have an important role in organising the exercise. Effective participation will require clear coordination, access to cleaning tools, protective equipment, waste collection services and suitable places for the disposal of collected rubbish.

Residents can contribute by cleaning around their homes and business premises, removing litter from public spaces where it is safe to do so, and ensuring that collected waste is placed in designated collection points. Businesses, schools, religious institutions and community groups can also organise teams to clean their compounds and nearby public areas.

Proper disposal is a critical part of the exercise. Moving rubbish from one location and leaving it in another does not solve the problem. Waste should be gathered, handled safely and taken to approved collection or disposal facilities through the arrangements made by the relevant local authorities.

Public urged to plan movements

Movement restrictions are expected to apply during the designated cleaning hours, with exemptions for essential services and other authorised categories. Members of the public should pay attention to instructions issued by the relevant authorities, plan necessary journeys in advance and cooperate with officials responsible for managing the exercise.

Anyone who needs to travel during the restricted period should confirm the applicable guidance and exemption arrangements before setting off. Essential medical needs and emergency services should be handled through the appropriate channels.

Clean surroundings must become a daily habit

Although the organised exercise is scheduled for three hours, lasting improvements will depend on what happens after the event. Households can reduce litter by using proper waste containers, while businesses and institutions can establish routine waste-management practices and discourage dumping in drainage channels, roadsides and open spaces.

Communities can also work with local leaders to identify recurring waste-disposal challenges, improve collection arrangements and encourage neighbours to maintain shared spaces. Consistent action is more effective than waiting for a designated cleaning day before addressing sanitation problems.

For residents of Mbale and other urban centres, the exercise offers an opportunity to focus on the cleanliness of markets, streets, drainage systems and neighbourhoods. Community participation, supported by reliable waste collection and proper disposal, will be essential if the effort is to produce visible and lasting results.

As Uganda prepares for Saturday’s exercise, the message is straightforward: cleaner communities require cooperation from everyone. Residents are encouraged to participate responsibly, follow local guidance and continue practising good sanitation beyond the scheduled hours.

KEY DETAILS
National Cleaning Day: Saturday, 31 October 2026
Time: 7:00 a.m. – 10:00 a.m.
Coverage: Communities across Uganda
Focus: Roadsides, markets, drainage channels, schools, health facilities, workplaces and other public spaces
